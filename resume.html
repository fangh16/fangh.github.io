Research Vision

Understanding emergence in complex systems

My research aims to understand how microscopic interactions give rise to collective structure, dynamics, and function. I ask what distinguishes complex systems from simpler ones, and which variables and correlations are needed to explain emergent behavior. The first two directions examine connections between equilibrium and nonequilibrium physics. The third explores how AI can help understand and control complex systems, and how their collective behavior can support information processing and learning.

Structural Evolution and Assembly Pathways

I study how frustration, reactions, and competing time scales shape structural evolution and assembly pathways under complex conditions. Systems of interest include frustrated crystallization, DNA reaction dynamics, and chemical or polymer co-assembly. Combining molecular dynamics, phase-field modeling, and experimental collaborations, I aim to connect equilibrium organizing principles with kinetic competition and history dependence, with potential applications in photonic crystals and mesoporous materials.

Driven Collective Dynamics

I explore how nonequilibrium driving changes many-body correlations and gives rise to collective dynamical behavior. I seek to identify when an equilibrium reference can describe structural statistics, relaxation, and response, and when new nonequilibrium descriptions are needed. Comparing the two provides a route to understanding the origins and limits of emergent collective behavior.

AI and Complex Systems

I investigate the two-way relationship between artificial intelligence and complex physical systems. AI offers tools to identify higher-order correlations, discover effective descriptions, and predict or control collective behavior. In the other direction, complex systems can provide physical processes for information processing and learning. This research aims to connect the understanding of emergence with the design and use of functional physical systems.
