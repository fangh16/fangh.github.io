Understanding emergence in complex systems

I am an assistant professor at the State Key Laboratory of Surface Physics, Fudan University. My research explores how microscopic interactions generate collective structures, dynamics, and functions. I study structural evolution and driven systems to connect equilibrium and nonequilibrium physics, and explore how AI and complex physical systems can advance one another.

Research Focus

My central goal is to understand emergence: how many interacting components produce collective behavior, and when that behavior admits a simple effective description. I combine experiments, simulations, and physical modeling to investigate the role of correlations, constraints, and driving.

Current Directions

  • Structural Evolution and Assembly Pathways.
  • Driven Collective Dynamics.
  • AI and Complex Systems.
